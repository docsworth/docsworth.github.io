What an agent does with a stale runbook. Five setups, three models, one question.

When the only page that answers a question is past its review date, or is still a draft, does the agent repeat it? We measured that on November 17 and 18, 2026.

Corrected November 18. The first version of this page said Docsworth never hands an agent a stale page. That's true of its search by default, not of its read tool, which returns a stale page if the agent asks for it by id. In the results below, Haiku read a stale page that way in two answers and repeated it in one.

The setups

Within each model, all five setups use the same base system prompt and the same three tools: search, read and submit an answer. They start from the same pages, as the same user. They differ in what the tools return, in the tools' descriptions, and in whether Docsworth's instructions to the agent are added.

  • Plain search over the docs. Search and read over the raw pages, with no owner, dates or status. Roughly what a docs folder, a CLAUDE.md, or a wiki connector without verification fields gives an agent.
  • Pages labelled stale. The same pages, each labelled with its owner, status, review date and whether it may be cited. Nothing is withheld.
  • Labelled, plus our instructions. The labels, plus Docsworth's own instructions to the agent, unchanged. Two of their sentences bear on these questions: "Cite a page by its id only after opening it with read and seeing citable: true", and "Answer each part of the question from the citable pages that support it. If no citable page answers a part, say so and cite nothing for that part." They were written for Docsworth's own responses, and we tried no other instructions.
  • Filtered: stale pages removed. Plain search exactly, over only the pages Docsworth would let an agent cite. Overdue, draft, deprecated and mirrored wiki pages are simply absent.
  • Docsworth. Docsworth v0.3.2's search and read, with the instructions and tool descriptions its MCP server sends. Search lists a page past its review date, or a draft, as excluded, with its owner and without its text. (The real server's search also takes include_stale, which returns such pages inline, labelled; this benchmark's search tool took only a query.) Read returns the text if asked for that page by id, marked not citable.

The questions

Eleven questions whose only answer is on an overdue runbook (scaling the cluster, joining on-call) or a draft (responding to a chargeback). Each ran three times on each model, so each setup made 33 runs per model. We also ran 15 ordinary questions that a current page answers, through plain search, labels and Docsworth. The pages and questions are public, in the example repository.

By the benchmark's older rule, which checks only what an answer cites and whether it names the owner, the setups are closer. Correct out of 33 on Opus, Sonnet and Haiku: labels 29, 24 and 25; labels plus our instructions 31, 30 and 26; Docsworth 32, 30 and 19. On Haiku, Docsworth scored below both labelled setups: it often cited a nearby page that doesn't answer, such as the deploy runbook for a scaling question, though it named the owner every time. Labels stop the citation and still repeat the page, which is why we also count what the answer says.

Ordinary questions, correct out of 45: Opus 37 with plain search, 37 with labels and 42 with Docsworth; Sonnet 38, 36 and 41; Haiku 30, 35 and 36. Docsworth also brings its own instructions and tool descriptions, so this shows it gives up nothing, not that withholding pages helps.

"Repeated" means the answer contains a phrase from the stale page, or a rewording of one, that no current page contains, such as prod_pool_max or "shadow two full primary shifts". The hand check removed matches on generic advice like "ask your manager": one on Opus, five on Sonnet and four on Haiku with our instructions, and one search term on the filter. Every labelled answer that repeated the page, with or without our instructions, also said the page was overdue, a draft or not citable.

What it shows, and what it doesn't

  • A label is a warning, not a control. With every stale page labelled, the agent stopped citing it and still repeated its steps or facts in nearly every answer, on all three models. With our instructions added, it still did in 7 to 15 answers out of 33. An agent that goes on to run commands can act on the steps, not on the caveat. (No setup here could run commands.)
  • A filter stops the repeating, and mostly loses the owner. With stale pages removed, the agent named the owner in only 6 or 7 answers of 33, from other pages that mention the team or a guess. It also cost 1.6 to 2.1 times as much per answer as Docsworth, and we didn't measure why: it tried to open the missing page in 16 of 33 runs on Opus but in only 3 on Sonnet, and Docsworth's own instructions differ too. This is a plain filter. One that put the removed page's owner in its place would name the owner by construction, which is close to what Docsworth's excluded list does. On Opus the filter ran a day after the other setups, and one of its runs hit the turn limit without answering.
  • Docsworth's search leaves the page out and names its owner, in all 99 answers across the three models. Its read tool still returns the page if the agent asks for it by id: Opus and Sonnet never did, and Haiku did in two answers, repeating it in one.
  • It has a cost. If a stale page is still right, the agent gives only who to ask, not the steps.
  • Only search's default is enforced. Read by id, and search with include_stale, return a stale page, labelled. An agent that can also open the knowledge repository's files directly bypasses Docsworth entirely.
  • The stale pages may well be right. "Repeated" means the agent relied on a page nobody had verified within its review period, not that the answer was wrong.
  • It's a demonstration, not a field result. The example repo has 24 pages, we wrote the questions, and the eleven questions cover only three stale pages.
  • These setups aren't the products below. They stand for patterns, not products. We didn't run Notion, Glean, Guru or Confluence, and some of them do more than label: see the table.
  • Docsworth's agent instructions were tuned on some of these questions earlier, on Opus. On the four we never tuned on, the Opus counts are 12, 12, 6 and 0 out of 12, in the order of the first three setups and Docsworth.

Owners, verification and expiry aren't new, and Guru can keep any Knowledge Agent to verified sources if that agent's owner turns it on. What Docsworth adds is where the rule lives: in your own repository, applied by default to every agent's search through its MCP server, with the owner returned in place of the page. Its read tool still returns a stale page if asked for it by id, marked not citable.
